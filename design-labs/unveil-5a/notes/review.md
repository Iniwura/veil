## Decision

Advance **A with Canvas2D**, with C retained as a serious alternative. Canvas's folds create a more readable material world than the raw WebGL shader in these renders. The shader's slightly lower JavaScript submission time is not proof of lower total cost and does not justify choosing it.

Reject B as the master direction. Retain its narrow diffraction/proof-edge experiment only as a reference; do not import its generic vault silhouette into production.

A and C are close, not a landslide. A better links ambient material to Save and Draw; C has stronger logo/silhouette recall and lower implementation complexity. Do not combine every interesting detail into one overloaded hybrid. The next review should choose one consistent material orientation.

## What still stops this being 10/10

- The shared reveal surface does not yet feel uniquely authored for each direction.
- Mobile Save is long: amount → six stages → private object needs tighter hierarchy.
- Small uppercase telemetry is overused in places, despite the calmer body typography.
- A's fold field can look like vertical ribbons rather than encrypted material.
- C's resting Draw is strong but its computation state needs a more specific internal rhythm.
- Logo proof lines at 16px need pixel-level optical adjustment before brand adoption.
- First-session choreography has been rendered in timed frames, not judged through a pristine recorded clip.
- All timings are concept timers. Production would need event-driven transitions and wallet-lifecycle focus management.
- Real mobile GPU, Safari, 200% zoom and screen-reader passes remain untested.

The wordmark-removal test is promising for A/C: asymmetric solids, narrow proof edge, sealed computation and release stroke form a recurring grammar. It is not fully passed across every shared scene yet. This lab is evidence for the next design decision, not permission to ship.
